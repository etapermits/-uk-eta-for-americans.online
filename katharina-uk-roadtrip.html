17 July — Dover to York, via Cambridge. Your first day. From the ferry, head north rather than through London — Cambridge sits conveniently on the way and is worth a few hours' wander before pushing on to York for the night.
17 Jul

Dover → Cambridge → York

~290 miles total · Arrive, wander, drive on

▾
🚢

Dover — you're driving on the LEFT

From the moment you leave the ferry, stay left. At roundabouts (frequent) go clockwise and give way to the right. With almost forty years of left-hand driving in New Zealand behind you, this should come back within the first few miles. Head north on the M20/M25/M11 towards Cambridge, skirting London rather than driving through it.

Drive left
🎓

Cambridge — a few hours' wander

Park on the outskirts (Park & Ride is easiest — city-centre parking is scarce and expensive) and walk in. The Backs — the green riverside walk behind King's, Trinity, and St John's Colleges — is the classic view of Cambridge and free to walk. King's College Chapel is a Gothic masterpiece, arguably the finest single building in Cambridge; punting on the River Cam past the colleges is touristy but genuinely lovely on a sunny afternoon. The Market Square in the centre has been trading since medieval times — good for a quick lunch.

York — your first night

An hour or two further north via the A1(M). York is the finest medieval city in England, founded by the Romans in 71 AD as Eboracum, later the Viking city of Jorvik. Every layer of its history is still visible in the streets. You'll pass back through here on the return leg of your own trip is not planned, so make the most of this one evening — a walk along a stretch of the City Walls or through the Shambles after the day-trippers have gone is a lovely way to end a long driving day.

MedievalOvernight stop
📍 Google Maps ↗
⛪

If you have a spare hour in York

York Minster — 252 years to build, the world's largest area of medieval stained glass in the Great East Window — is close to wherever you'll be staying and worth even a brief look before bed or over breakfast. The Shambles, a medieval street so narrow the upper floors nearly touch overhead, is two minutes from the Minster and said to have inspired Diagon Alley.

Gothic cathedral
18–19 July — York to Edinburgh, along the coast, then a full day. Take the coast road north where you can rather than the inland A1 — the Northumberland coastline (Bamburgh, Holy Island/Lindisfarne if the tide table allows, Berwick-upon-Tweed) is spectacular and largely overlooked by drivers rushing straight up the motorway.

Bamburgh Castle & Holy Island (Lindisfarne)

Bamburgh Castle sits dramatically on a basalt outcrop above a huge, near-empty beach — one of the great views of the English coast. Holy Island (Lindisfarne) is tidal — the causeway floods twice a day, so check the safe crossing times before you detour out; if the tide is against you, admire it from the mainland instead and keep moving.

Edinburgh Castle

Perched on an ancient volcanic plug 430 feet above the city. Inside: the Scottish Crown Jewels, the Stone of Destiny, and the 15th-century cannon Mons Meg. Book tickets in advance — July queues without a booking are brutal. Allow 2–3 hours.

The Royal Mile & the Closes

Runs from the Castle down to the Palace of Holyroodhouse. The real discoveries are the closes — narrow medieval alleyways off both sides. Mary King's Close can be visited on a guided tour; Riddle's Court and White Horse Close are among the best.

Loch Ness

23 miles long, 755 feet deep, more fresh water than all the lakes in England and Wales combined — its peat-dark water is why Nessie has stayed so elusive. Urquhart Castle on the western shore is the classic viewpoint. The southern shore road (B862) is quieter and just as dramatic.

Glencoe

Drive slowly, stop, walk. A glacial valley of extraordinary drama — the Three Sisters dropping straight to the valley floor. Site of the 1692 Glencoe Massacre; the visitor centre explains it well.

Ben Nevis & Fort William

The reason for the whole trip

🏔️

Ben Nevis (1,345m)

Britain's highest mountain. The Mountain Track starts at the Ben Nevis Visitor Centre — 17km return, 1,345m of ascent, allow 7–9 hours. Start before 8am. Proper hiking boots, waterproofs, warm layers, 2 litres of water minimum, and food. Use one of your two full days here for the climb and keep the other as a weather buffer or for a gentler day around the town and Loch Linnhe.

SummitFull day hike
⚠️ Ben Nevis safety: Check the mountain forecast at mwis.org.uk the day before. Tell your accommodation your intended route and expected return. The summit can be 15°C colder than Fort William, and often in cloud even in July.

Scottish midges

Present in vast numbers near water and vegetation from June to August, worst at dawn and dusk. Buy Smidge or Avon Skin So Soft before you arrive — it genuinely works. Glasgow — a driving stop, not a destination today

You'll pass through rather than linger, but if you want an hour, the Kelvingrove Art Gallery is free and excellent, and the West End around Byres Road has good coffee for a break. Otherwise treat Glasgow as the halfway point of a long day and push on south.

🧱

Hadrian's Wall

Built from 122 AD to mark the northern frontier of the Roman Empire — 73 miles coast to coast, and the best-preserved stretches are right around Carlisle and Housesteads to the east. Housesteads Roman Fort is the most complete fort on the whole wall, with dramatic views along the ridge the wall follows. Birdoswald Roman Fort, closer to Carlisle, is quieter and equally rewarding, with a good stretch of wall you can walk along. Even an hour walking a section of the wall at golden hour is worth the detour.

Camping near Carlisle — weather permitting

There are decent campsites close to Hadrian's Wall and the Eden Valley around Carlisle, with easy access to the wall itself. Caernarfon — your base for the night

Edward I built the castle in 1283 to control Wales — staggering in scale. Near the castle, a street lined with independent coffee shops and cafés is the perfect end to a driving day. The town walls are largely intact and walkable, and the Welsh language is very much alive here.

Mount Snowdon (1,085m) — 27 July, if possible

The Llanberis Path is the most popular route — 5 miles each way, allow 5–6 hours return — or take the historic rack railway to the summit if you'd rather save your legs after Ben Nevis. On a clear day you can see Ireland. Given you've already done Ben Nevis, this is very achievable, and the summit café (Hafod Eryri) is a genuinely well-designed modern building.

🚗 Driving on the left

  • Stay left at all times. At every junction look right first — traffic comes from your right
  • Roundabouts are everywhere. Go clockwise. Give way to traffic already on the roundabout (from your right)
  • In Scotland and Wales you'll meet single-track roads with passing places — pull into one to let oncoming traffic pass, and a wave as they go is expected
  • Speed limits: 30mph in towns, 60mph on single-carriageway A-roads, 70mph on motorways — speed cameras are common on A-roads through villages
  • UK petrol stations: unleaded (petrol) is the green nozzle, diesel is the black nozzle

☔ Weather

  • July is Britain's best month, but expect some rain — the west (Wales, the Lakes, Scotland) is wetter than the east
  • A waterproof jacket in the car at all times, non-negotiable
  • Mountain weather changes fast — check mwis.org.uk before Ben Nevis and Snowdon, and again for your Carlisle camping decision
  • Scottish midges are worst at dawn and dusk near water — buy Smidge repellent before entering the Highlands

🏨 Accommodation & bookings

  • Fort William is already booked — good. Keep booking 2–3 nights ahead for the rest, especially Edinburgh and the Lakes
  • You've already noticed the Highlands are busy this July — the same applies to camping near Hadrian's Wall, so have a Carlisle fallback in mind
  • Wild camping is legal in Scotland under the Land Reform Act 2003 — not in England or Wales without landowner permission
  • In Edinburgh and York, park outside the centre and walk in — city-centre parking is expensive and scarce

💷 Money & practicalities

  • Currency: Pound Sterling (£) throughout, including Scotland (Scottish banknotes valid in Scotland, may be refused in England)
  • Contactless payment accepted virtually everywhere
  • Tipping: 10–15% in restaurants if service isn't included; not expected in pubs
  • Tap water is safe everywhere in the UK

⛰️ Ben Nevis & Snowdon — preparation checklist

  • Start before 8am from the trailhead car park
  • Proper hiking boots, not trainers
  • Waterproof jacket and warm layers even if it's warm and sunny at the bottom
  • 2 litres of water per person minimum, plus food
  • Check the mountain forecast the evening before
  • Tell your accommodation your route and expected return time

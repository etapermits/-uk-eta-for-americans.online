Arrival Evening — Royal Mile & The Witchery

Check in · first impressions · dinner

▾
🏨

Check in & freshen up

You land at 4:30pm. Allow an hour to get into the city. Drop your bags and head straight out — Edinburgh on a summer evening is magical.

🕯️

The Witchery by the Castle

At the very top of the Royal Mile, in the shadow of Edinburgh Castle, sits The Witchery — one of the most atmospheric restaurants in Britain. Gothic décor, candlelight, dark wood panelling, suits of armour. The area around it — Castlehill — has been the site of witch burnings, executions, and royal processions for 500 years. Even if you don't eat here, walk the closes (alleyways) around it after dark. Spine-tingling.

🚶

Evening walk — Royal Mile to Holyrood

Walk the full length of the Royal Mile downhill from the Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse (the King's Scottish residence). At night the street is lit, buskers play, and the closes off both sides beckon. Wander into at least three — each has a story. Look for Advocates Close and Mary King's Close.

Edinburgh Castle + Greyfriars + Harry Potter Tour + Ghost Bus

The ultimate Harry Potter & dark history day

▾
🏰

Edinburgh Castle — 9am (book ahead!)

Open at 9:30am — arrive early before the crowds. The Scottish Crown Jewels, the Stone of Destiny, Mons Meg (a 15th century cannon), and views over the entire city. Allow 2.5 hours inside. The castle has been continuously occupied for over 3,000 years — the oldest building inside dates to the 12th century.

Must-seeBook ahead

⚡

Greyfriars Kirkyard — the real Harry Potter graveyard

J.K. Rowling used this churchyard for inspiration. Look for the gravestone of Thomas Riddell — yes, that Riddell. And William McGonagall (Professor McGonagall). The grave of Greyfriars Bobby — the loyal Skye Terrier who guarded his owner's grave for 14 years — is just outside the gates. The kirkyard is genuinely atmospheric, especially on a grey Edinburgh day. Free to enter.

Getyourguide.com and Airbnb Experiences both list good options. Book for early afternoon after the castle.

The wild heart of Scotland — a full day out

▾
🚌

Loch Ness, Glencoe & Highlands Tour — book with Rabbies or Timberbush

Several excellent operators run this as a full day from Edinburgh — departing around 8am, returning around 8-9pm. You'll see Glencoe (one of Scotland's most dramatic and haunting glens — site of the 1692 massacre), Loch Ness (vast, dark, and Nessie-inhabited as far as anyone can prove), Urquhart Castle ruins on the loch shore, and the Highland scenery that makes Scotland unlike anywhere else in the world. Your group circled this one — don't miss it.

Stockbridge Market + National Museum + Royal Botanic Garden

A relaxed and beautiful day

▾
🥕

Stockbridge Market — Sunday morning

One of Edinburgh's best-loved markets — running every Sunday in the charming village-within-a-city neighbourhood of Stockbridge. Artisan food, local produce, crafts, street food. The neighbourhood itself is gorgeous — Georgian townhouses, the Water of Leith running through it, independent shops and cafés. Go hungry. August 7 is a Friday — check if the market runs on Fridays too, otherwise adjust the day order.

🏛️

National Museum of Scotland — free

One of the finest free museums in Europe. The history of Scotland from Pictish stones to Dolly the sheep — the world's first cloned mammal, whose actual preserved body is on display here. The Victorian Grand Gallery is a breathtaking space. Allow 2-3 hours. The rooftop terrace has spectacular views over the Old Town skyline.

Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh — free

70 acres of beautifully maintained gardens just north of the city centre — free to enter. In August the gardens are at their peak. The glasshouses contain plants from every climate on earth. The views back over the city from the upper part of the garden are outstanding. A perfect afternoon wind-down after the museum.

Calton Hill + Camera Obscura + Tartan Weaving Mill

Views · optical illusions · Scottish keepsakes

▾
⛰️

Calton Hill — morning

A short walk from the city centre — a hill topped with monuments including an unfinished replica of the Parthenon (they ran out of money in 1829 and it's been "unfinished" ever since — now a beloved Edinburgh landmark). The 360° views from the top take in the castle, Arthur's Seat, the Firth of Forth, and the New Town below. Free, always open, 15 minutes to walk up.

🔭

Camera Obscura & World of Illusion

At the top of the Royal Mile, next to the castle — a Victorian optical instrument that projects a live 360° image of the city onto a viewing table. Surrounding it are five floors of optical illusions, holograms, and mind-bending exhibits. Great fun for everyone. The rooftop terrace has the best views of the castle esplanade. Your group circled this one — it delivers.

Tartan Weaving Mill

Right at the top of the Royal Mile — you can watch tartan being woven on historic looms, learn about Scottish clan tartans (find out if Melvin or Christie have Scottish heritage — you might be surprised), and browse an extraordinary range of Scottish textiles, kilts, and gifts. A genuine working mill, not just a gift shop.

Platform 9¾ — King's Cross Station

You arrive at King's Cross — and Platform 9¾ is right there in the station concourse. The Harry Potter Shop, the luggage trolley disappearing into the wall, the photo opportunity. Free to visit, shop obviously costs money. Don't skip it just because you've just arrived — it's right there.

Borough Market — London's greatest food market

The oldest food market in London — on this site for over 1,000 years, under the railway arches of London Bridge. Open Monday to Saturday. In August it's buzzing. Every cuisine, every artisan food you can imagine — Spanish charcuterie, French cheese, Ethiopian stew, British pies, fresh oysters, exotic street food. Go hungry and graze your way through. The area around it (Bermondsey Street, Maltby Street) is equally good.

Harry Potter Warner Bros Studio Tour

The full day — absolutely non-negotiable

▾
🎬

Warner Bros Studio Tour — The Making of Harry Potter

The actual studios where all 8 Harry Potter films were made — in Leavesden, Hertfordshire, 20 miles from central London. The Great Hall, Dumbledore's office, the Gryffindor common room, Diagon Alley, the Forbidden Forest, Platform 9¾ (the real one), the full-scale Hogwarts model, the original costumes, props, and sets. Butterbeer in the backlot. This is not a theme park replica — these are the actual sets. Allow a full day — most people spend 4-5 hours inside. Take the official shuttle from Watford Junction (train from Euston, 20 minutes).

Jack the Ripper Tour + London Mithraeum + Tower of London

London's darkest history day

▾
🔪

Jack the Ripper Walking Tour — evening

The East End of London — Whitechapel, 1888. Five murders, no solution, 135 years of fascination. The walking tours take you through the actual streets where the murders happened — many of the buildings and alleyways still exist. The best operators (Look Up London, Ripper Vision) combine genuine historical research with compelling storytelling. An evening tour is the authentic experience — the streets feel genuinely different after dark. Your group circled this one — it delivers.

Dark historyBook ahead
📍 Google Maps ↗
🏛️

London Mithraeum — hidden Roman temple

Discovered in 1954 during post-war rebuilding — a 3rd century Roman temple to the god Mithras, buried 20 feet below the modern city. Now preserved in the basement of a Bloomberg building in the City of London, with an extraordinary immersive experience recreating the original rituals. Free to visit (book a timed slot). One of London's most extraordinary hidden secrets — most Londoners have never been.

Roman LondonFree
📍 Google Maps ↗
🗼

Tower of London

Nearly 1,000 years of history on the north bank of the Thames. The Crown Jewels (the actual crowns, orbs, and sceptres used at coronations), the Yeoman Warder (Beefeater) guided tours, the White Tower (the original Norman keep), and the site of more royal executions than anywhere else in England — including Anne Boleyn and Lady Jane Grey. Allow 3 hours. Book ahead in August.

Leeds Castle & Canterbury Day Trip

Out of London · medieval splendour

▾
🏰

Leeds Castle, Kent

Often called "the loveliest castle in the world" — and it might be. Built on two islands in a lake, surrounded by 500 acres of parkland in the Kent countryside. Henry VIII used it as a palace. The grounds include a yew maze, a grotto, an aviary, and gardens that are extraordinary in August. About an hour from London by train and bus. Allow 3-4 hours at the castle.

CastleGardens
📍 Google Maps ↗
⛪

Canterbury Cathedral

A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the oldest and most famous Christian structures in England — the mother church of the Anglican Communion worldwide. Thomas Becket was murdered here in 1170 by knights acting for Henry II — his shrine drew pilgrims from across Europe for centuries (the Canterbury Tales tells their stories). The medieval stained glass is extraordinary. About 30 minutes from Leeds Castle by train.

Camden Market + Kyoto Garden + The Painted Hall

Markets · hidden Japanese garden · baroque masterpiece

▾
🛍️

Camden Market — morning

London's most famous alternative market — sprawling through warehouses and lock-ups along the Regent's Canal. Street food from every corner of the world, vintage clothing, independent designers, music memorabilia, tattoo studios, and a energy that is entirely its own. The Stables Market (inside the old horse hospital) is the most atmospheric section. Go before noon before it gets too crowded.

🌸

Kyoto Garden, Holland Park — hidden gem

A traditional Japanese garden donated by the city of Kyoto to London in 1991 — tucked inside Holland Park in Kensington. A waterfall, koi carp, stone lanterns, manicured trees, and peacocks wandering freely. Almost nobody outside London knows it exists. On a sunny August afternoon it is genuinely magical — a pocket of Japan in the middle of the city. Free to enter.

Hidden gemFree
📍 Google Maps ↗
🎨

The Painted Hall, Old Royal Naval College, Greenwich

Called "the Sistine Chapel of the UK" — and the comparison is not an exaggeration. Sir James Thornhill's vast ceiling and wall paintings took 19 years to complete (1707-1726) and cover 40,000 square feet. Nelson's body lay in state here before his funeral. The building sits in the extraordinary baroque complex of the Old Royal Naval College on the Thames at Greenwich — free to enter the grounds, small charge for the Painted Hall. Worth every penny.

🚇 Getting around London

  • Get an Oyster card or use a contactless bank card — tap in and out on the Underground, buses, and Overground. Never buy single paper tickets, they cost double
  • The Underground (the Tube) is the fastest way across the city — Google Maps gives perfect real-time directions
  • Black cabs are brilliant but expensive — Uber works well and is cheaper
  • The Elizabeth line (opened 2022) connects Heathrow, Paddington, and central London in minutes
  • Buses are cheap and you see the city — bus 11 goes past St Paul's, along the Strand, and past the Houses of Parliament

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Edinburgh practicalities

  • The Old Town is hilly — comfortable shoes are essential, especially on the cobbles
  • August is Edinburgh Festival season — the city is packed, buzzing, and has street performers everywhere. Book accommodation and restaurants ahead
  • The Edinburgh Fringe (world's largest arts festival) runs all of August — free shows on the Royal Mile every day, ticketed shows in venues across the city
  • Weather: bring a waterproof layer. Edinburgh in August can be glorious or grey — often both in the same day

💷 Money

  • Currency: Pound Sterling (£) throughout — both England and Scotland
  • Contactless payment accepted virtually everywhere — you rarely need cash
  • Tipping: 10-15% in restaurants. Not expected in pubs or cafés
  • Free attractions in London: National Museum, Tate Modern, British Museum, National Gallery, South Bank walk — all completely free
